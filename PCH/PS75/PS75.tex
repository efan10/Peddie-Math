\problem{1}
If $V$ is the volume of a cube with edge length $x$ and the cube expands as time passes, find $\dfrac{dV}{dt}$ in terms of $\dfrac{dx}{dt}$. \vem

Given: $x $

Unknown: $\dfrac{dV}{dt}$
\begin{gather*}
    V=x^3\\
    \dfrac{dV}{dt}=\boxed{3x^2\frac{dx}{dt}}
\end{gather*}

\problem{2}
Each side of a square is increasing at a rate of 6 cm/s. At what rate is the area of the square increasing when the area of the square is 16 cm$^2$? \vem

Given: $\dfrac{ds}{dt}=6$ cm/s \vem

Unknown: $\dfrac{dA}{dt} \Big|_{A=16}$
\begin{gather*}
    A=s^2\\
    \frac{dA}{dt}=2s\cdot \frac{ds}{dt}=2\cdot 4\cdot 6=\boxed{48\text{ cm$^2$/s}}
\end{gather*}
The area of the square is increasing at a rate of 48 cm$^2$/s.

\problem{4}
Suppose $y=x^3+2x$, where $x$ and $y$ are functions of $t$. If $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=5$, find $\dfrac{dy}{dt}$ when $x=2$. \vem

Given: $y=x^3+2x$, $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=5$ \vem

Unknown: $\dfrac{dy}{dt} \Big|_{x=2}$
\begin{gather*}
    \dydt=3x^2\cdot \dxdt+2\cdot \dxdt=3\cdot 4\cdot 5+2\cdot 5=\boxed{70}
\end{gather*}

\problem{6}
A plane flying horizontally at an altitude of 1 mi and a speed of 500 mi/h passes directly over a radar station. Find the rate at which the distance from the plane to the station is increasing when it is 2 mi away from the station. \vem

Given: $h=1$ mi, $\dxdt=500$ mi/h\vem

Unknown: $\dydt \Big|_{x= 2}$
\begin{gather*}
    y^2=h^2+x^2=1+x^2\\
    2y\cdot \dydt=2x\cdot \dxdt\\
    \dydt=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\cdot  \dxdt=\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\cdot 500=\boxed{200\sqrt{5} \text{ mi/h}}
\end{gather*}
The distance from the plane to the station is increasing at a rate of $200\sqrt{5} \text{ mi/h}$.


\problem{8}
Two cars start moving from the same point. One travels south at 60mi/h and the other travels west at 25mi/h. At what rate is the distance between the cars increasing two hours later? \vem

Given: $\dxdt=60$ mi/h, $\dydt=25$ mi/h\\

Unknown: $\dfrac{dz}{dt} \Big|_{t=2 \text{h}}$
\begin{gather*}
    z^2=x^2+y^2\\
    x=\dxdt\cdot t=120 mi\\
    y=\dydt \cdot t=50mi\\
    2z\cdot \dfrac{dz}{dt}=2x\cdot \dxdt+2y\cdot \dydt\\
    \dfrac{dz}{dt}=\frac{x\cdot \dxdt+y\cdot \dydt}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}=\frac{120\cdot 60+25\cdot 50}{130}=\boxed{65 \text{ mi/h}}
\end{gather*}
The distance between the cars increases at a rate of 65 \text{mi/h}.

\problem{10}
The altitude of a triangle is increasing at a rate of 1 cm/min while the area of the triangle is increasing at a rate of 2 cm$^2$/min. At what rate is the base of the triangle changing when the altitude is 10 cm and the area is 100 cm$^2$?\vem

Given: $\dfrac{dh}{dt}=1$ cm/min, $\dfrac{dA}{dt}=2$ cm$^2$/min. \vem

Unknown: $\dfrac{dB}{dt}\Big|_{h=10, A=100}$
\begin{gather*}
    B=\frac{2A}{h}\\
    \frac{dB}{dt}=\frac{2(h\cdot \frac{dA}{dt}-A\cdot\frac{dh}{dt})}{h^2}=\frac{2(10\cdot 2-100 \cdot 1)}{100}=\boxed{-1.6 \text{ cm/min}}
\end{gather*}
The base of the triangle changes at a rate of $-1.6 \text{ cm/min}$.

\problem{12}
Water is leaking out of an inverted conical tank at a rate of 10,000 cm$^3$/min at the same time that water is being pumped into the tank at a constant rate. The tank has height 6 m and the diameter at the top is 4 m. If the water level is rising at a rate of 20 cm/min when the height of the water is 2m, find the rate at which water is being pumped into the tank. \vem

Given: $h=6$m, $\dfrac{dV_1}{dt}=-10000$ cm$^3$/min, $\dfrac{dh}{dt} \Big|_{h=2}=20$\\

Unknown: $\dfrac{dV_2}{dt}\Big|_{h=2}$
\begin{gather*}
    V=\frac{1}{3}\pi (\frac{h}{3})^2h=\frac{\pi h^3}{27}\\
    \frac{dV_{net}}{dt}=\frac{\pi h^2}{9}\cdot \frac{dh}{dt}=\frac{\pi \cdot (200)^2 \cdot 20}{9}=\frac{800000\pi}{9}\approx 279252\\
    \frac{dV_2}{dt}=\frac{dV_{net}}{dt}-\frac{dV_1}{dt}=279252-(-10000)=\boxed{289252 $ cm$^3$/min$}
\end{gather*}
Water is being pumped into the tank at a rate of 289252 cm$^3$/min.

\problem{14}
Gravel is being dumped from a conveyor belt at a rate of 30 ft$^3$/min and its coarseness is such that it forms a pile in the shape of a cone whose base diameter and height are always equal. How fast is the height of the pile increasing when the pile is 10 ft hight? \vem

Given: $\dfrac{dV}{dt}=30$ ft$^3$/min\vem

Unknown: $\dfrac{dx}{dt} \Big|_{x=10}$
\begin{gather*}
    V=\frac{1}{3}\pi(\frac{x}{2})^2x=\frac{\pi x^3}{12}\\
    \frac{dV}{dt}=\frac{\pi x^2}{4}\cdot \dxdt\\
    \dxdt=\frac{\dfrac{dV}{dt}\cdot4}{\pi x^2}=\frac{30\cdot 4}{\pi \cdot 10^2}=\boxed{\frac{5}{6\pi}$ ft/min$}
\end{gather*}
The height of the pile increases at a rate of $\dfrac{5}{6\pi}$ ft/min.

\problem{16}
The Boyle's Law states that when a sample of gas is compressed at a constant temperature, the pressure $P$ and the volume $V$ satisfy the equation $PV=C$, where $C$ is a constant. Suppose that at a certain instant the volume is 600 cm$^3$, the pressures is 150 KPa, and the pressure is increasing at a arate of 20 KPa/min. At what rate is the volume decreasing at this instant? \vem

Given: $\dfrac{dP}{dt}=20$ KPa/min \vem

Unknown: $\dfrac{dV}{dt}\Big|_{V=600, P=150}$
\begin{gather*}
    V=\frac{C}{P}\\
    \frac{dV}{dt}=C\cdot (-\frac{1}{P^2})\cdot \frac{dP}{dt}=-\frac{V}{P}\cdot \frac{dP}{dt}=-\frac{600}{150}\cdot 20=\boxed{-80 $ cm$^3$/min$}
\end{gather*}
The volume decreases at a rate of $-80$ cm$^3$/min.

\problem{17}
If two resistors with resistances $R_1$ and $R_2$ are connected in parallel, then the total resistance $R$, measured
in ohms $(\Omega)$, is given by:
\[
\frac{1}{R}=\frac{1}{R_1}+\frac{1}{R_2}
\]
If $R_1$ and $R_2$ are increasing at a rate of 0.3 $\Omega$/s, respectively, how fast is $R$ changing when $R_1 = 80\Omega$
and $R_2 = 100\Omega$? \vem

Given: $\dfrac{dR_1}{dt}=\dfrac{dR_2}{dt}=0.3 \Omega$/s\vem

Unknown: $\dfrac{dR}{dt}\Big|_{R_1 = 80\Omega, R_2 = 100\Omega}$
\begin{gather*}
    R=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{R_1}+\frac{1}{R_2}}\\
    \frac{dR}{dt}=-\frac{-\frac{1}{R_1^2}\cdot\frac{dR_1}{dt}-\frac{1}{R_2^2}\cdot \frac{dR_2}{dt}}{(\frac{1}{R_1}+\frac{1}{R_2})^2}\\
    \frac{dR}{dt}=\frac{\frac{0.3}{80^2}+\frac{0.3}{100^2}}{(\frac{1}{80}+\frac{1}{100})^2}\\
    \boxed{\frac{dR}{dt}=\frac{41}{270}\Omega$/s$}
\end{gather*}
$R$ increases at a rate of $\dfrac{41}{270}\Omega$/s.

\problem{18}
Two sides of a triangle have lengths 12 m and 15 m. The angle between them is increasing at a rate of
2$^\circ$/min. How fast is the length of the third side increasing when the angle between the sides of fixed
length is 60$^\circ$? \vem

Given: $\dfrac{d\theta}{dt}=2^\circ$/min, $a=12$ m, $b=15$ m \vem

Unknown: $\dfrac{dx}{dt}\Big|_{\theta = 60^\circ}$
\begin{gather*}
    x^2=a^2+b^2-2ab \cos \theta\\
    x=\sqrt{12^2+15^2-2 \cdot 12\cdot 15\cdot \frac{1}{2}}=3\sqrt{21}\\
    2x\cdot \frac{dx}{dt}= 2ab\sin \theta \frac{d\theta}{dt}\\
    \dxdt=\frac{ab\sin \theta \frac{d\theta}{dt}}{x}=\frac{12\cdot 15\cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi}{90}}{3\sqrt{21}}=\boxed{\frac{\pi}{3\sqrt{7}} $ m/min$}
\end{gather*}
The length of the third side increases at a rate of $\dfrac{\pi}{3\sqrt{7}}$ m/min.

\problem{19}
The television camera is positioned 4000 ft from the base of a rocket launching pad. Let’s assume the
rocket rises vertically and its speed is 600 ft/s when it has risen 3000 ft. \vem

\subproblem{a}
How fast is the distance from the television camera to the rocket changing at that moment?
\begin{gather*}
    D^2=x^2+y^2\\
    2d\cdot \frac{dD}{dt}=2y\cdot \dydt\\
    \frac{dD}{dt}=\frac{2\cdot3000\cdot 600}{2\cdot 5000}\\
    \boxed{\frac{dD}{dt}=360 $ ft/s$}
\end{gather*}
The distance is increasing at a rate of 360 ft/s. \vem

\subproblem{b}
If the television camera is always kept aimed at the rocket, how fast is the camera’s angle of elevation changing at that same moment?
\begin{gather*}
    \tan \theta =\frac{y}{x}\\
    \theta =\arctan \frac{y}{x}\\
    \frac{d\theta}{dt}=\frac{1}{1+(\frac{y}{x})^2}\cdot \dydt \cdot \frac{1}{x}\\
    \frac{d\theta}{dt}=\frac{1}{1+(\frac{3}{4})^2} \cdot \frac{600}{4000}\\
    \boxed{\frac{d\theta}{dt}=\frac{12}{125} $ rads/s$}
\end{gather*}
The camera's angle of elevation is increasing at a rate of $\dfrac{12}{125}$ rads/s.

\problem{20}
A plane flies horizontally at an altitude of 5 km and passes directly over a tracking telescope on the
ground. When the angle of elevation is $\pi$/3, this angle is decreasing at a rate of $\pi$/6 rad/min. How fast
is the plane traveling at that time? \vem

Given: $h=5$ km, $\dfrac{d\theta}{dt}=-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ rad/min\vem

Unknown: $\dxdt \Big|_{\theta = \frac{\pi}{3}}$
\begin{gather*}
    \tan \theta =\frac{h}{x} \implies x=\frac{5}{\sqrt{3}}\\
    \sec^2\theta \cdot \frac{d\theta}{dt}=h\cdot (-\frac{1}{x^2})\cdot \dxdt\\
    \dxdt=-\frac{d\theta}{dt}\cdot \frac{x^2\sec^2\theta }{h}=\frac{\pi}{6}\cdot \frac{25}{5\cdot3}\cdot 4=\boxed{\frac{10\pi}{9} $ km/min$}    
\end{gather*}
The plane is traveling at a velocity of $\dfrac{10\pi}{9}$ km/min.

\problem{21}
A plane flying with a constant speed of 300 km/h passes over a ground radar station at an altitude of
1km and climbs at an angle of $30^\circ$. At what rate is the distance from the plane to the radar station increasing a minute later? \vem

Given: $v=300$ km/h, $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ \vem

Unknown: $\dfrac{dD}{dt} \Big|_{t=1}$
\begin{gather*}
    D^2=x^2+y^2\\
    D^2=(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cdot 5t)^2+(1+\frac{1}{2}\cdot 5t)^2\\
    2D\cdot \frac{dD}{dt}=5\sqrt{3}t\cdot \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2}+(2+5t)\cdot \frac{5}{2}\\
    \frac{dD}{dt}=\frac{5\sqrt{3}t\cdot \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2}+(2+5t)\cdot \frac{5}{2}}{2\sqrt{(\frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2})^2+(\frac{7}{2})^2}}\\
    \boxed{\frac{dD}{dt}=\frac{55}{2\sqrt{31}} $ km/min$}
\end{gather*}
The distance from the plane to the radar station increases at a rate of $\dfrac{55}{2\sqrt{31}}$ km/min.

\problem{22}
A runner sprints around a circular track of radius 100 m at a constant speed of 7 m/sec. The runner’s
friend is standing at a distance 200 m from the center of the track. How fast is the distance between the
friends changing when the distance between them is 200 m? \vem

Given: $r=100, \dxdt=7$ \vem

Unknown: $\dfrac{dD}{dt} \Big|_{D=200}$
\begin{gather*}
    D^2=x^2+200^2-2\cdot 200 x \cos \theta\\
    40000=50000-40000\cos \theta \implies \cos \theta =\frac{1}{4}\\
    2D \cdot \frac{dD}{dt} = 400 x \sin \theta \cdot \frac{d\theta}{dt}\\
    \dxdt = 7 \implies \frac{d\theta}{dt}=\frac{7}{100}\\
    \frac{dD}{dt}= 100\cdot \frac{7}{100}\cdot \frac{\sqrt{15}}{4}\\
    \boxed{\frac{dD}{dt}=\frac{7\sqrt{15}}{4} $ m/s$}
\end{gather*}
The distance between the friends are changing at a rate of $\dfrac{7\sqrt{15}}{4}$ m/s.
